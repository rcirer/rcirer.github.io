The four muscle excursion ranges

EXCURSE · animated schematic

What it shows

Four muscles on one horizontal length axis, all fixed at the same origin on the left. Each is a belly joined by short tendons to two attachment points: a fixed proximal one and a distal one that travels. From the distal attachment an elastic band runs to a fixed anchor, so every muscle works against a resistance.

Two vertical lines mark the shortening limit — the shortest length the muscle reaches voluntarily — and the lengthening limit, the longest. A dashed line between them marks resting length. The bar under each muscle is its muscle excursion range: how far its distal attachment travels.

  • Inner MER works against the shortening limit and stays shorter than resting length.
  • Outer MER works against the lengthening limit and stays longer than resting length.
  • Mid MER straddles resting length without reaching either limit.
  • Complete MER travels the whole distance, limit to limit.

All four bands are at rest, with the same spacing between coils, when their muscle is at its longest; each then stretches by exactly the length of its own excursion. Read physically: the same band in different lengths. The belly thickens as it shortens and thins as it lengthens — volume is conserved — while the tendons keep their length. The four rows run in step, so at any instant the difference you see is the condition, not the phase. The cycle is 6 s split 3:1 between the eccentric and concentric halves.

The figure is schematic, not measured. The positions of the limits and of resting length are drawing choices. One of them has a visible consequence worth knowing before reusing the figure: placing resting length at 42 % of the voluntary range leaves Inner MER entirely below it and Outer MER entirely above it. In a real muscle that split is not guaranteed. The four ranges are anchored to the two limits and individualised per participant, muscle and exercise.
